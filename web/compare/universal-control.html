Glasspad vs Universal Control

Universal Control does not work with iPhone. It lets one Mac's keyboard and trackpad control up to two other Macs or iPads nearby (macOS 12.4 and iPadOS 15.4 or later, signed in to the same Apple Account). iPhone Mirroring goes the other way: your Mac controls your iPhone. To control a Mac from an iPhone you need an app. Glasspad is a free, open source one that makes the iPhone a trackpad, keyboard, remote and button deck for the Mac over Wi-Fi.

Which one fits

Choose Universal Control if

  • You have an iPad or a second Mac on your desk.
  • You want one keyboard and trackpad across them, with drag and drop.

Choose Glasspad if

  • You want your iPhone to control your Mac.
  • You want a remote for the couch, a deck of buttons, or a spare trackpad.

They work side by side: Glasspad does not replace Universal Control.

Questions

Does Universal Control work with iPhone?
No. Apple lists Macs and iPads only. For the iPhone, Apple points to iPhone Mirroring, which lets your Mac control your iPhone.
What is iPhone Mirroring?
A macOS 15 feature that shows your iPhone's screen on your Mac so you can use its apps there. It needs iOS 18, a Mac with Apple silicon or the T2 chip, and the same Apple Account.
How do I control my Mac from my iPhone?
With an app on both devices. Glasspad is free and open source: install it on the Mac, pair the iPhone by QR code, and use it as a trackpad, keyboard or remote over Wi-Fi.
Does Glasspad need an Apple Account?
Only a free Apple ID to install the app on your iPhone. Glasspad itself has no account, and the iPhone and Mac talk only over your Wi-Fi.

Install

Glasspad is free and open source (MIT). It needs a Mac on macOS 14 or later and an iPhone on iOS 18 or later, on the same Wi-Fi. To install, you also need Xcode 26 or later (free on the App Store), a cable for the first install, and a free Apple ID. Paste this into Terminal on your Mac:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Soham109/glasspad/main/install.sh | bash

It waits for you where Apple needs a click. With a free Apple ID, apps you install yourself stop opening after 7 days; your Mac renews Glasspad over Wi-Fi before then. Every step, with pictures: Install. Help: Guide.
