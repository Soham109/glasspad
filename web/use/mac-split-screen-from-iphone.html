Split screen on your Mac from your iPhone

On a Mac you split the screen from the green button at the top left of a window, or, on macOS 15 and later, by pressing fn, control and the left or right arrow. Glasspad puts this on your iPhone: tap Split Screen, choose Left & Right, Top & Bottom, Quarters or Full Screen (Split View), pick the windows and the display, and see where each one will go before anything moves. Window buttons put the front window on a half or a quarter, Fill or Center.

Install Glasspad Actions in the guide

How Glasspad does it

Window buttons

  • Split Screen opens Arrange: Left & Right, Top & Bottom, Quarters or Full Screen.
  • Left Half, Right Half, Top Half, Bottom Half, the four quarters, Fill and Center.
  • Next Display moves the front window to your other display.
  • Add them to Pinned from the Library, or keep them in a folder as here.

Displays

  • Touch and hold any window for the halves, Fill, Center and Split With.
  • Drag a window to another display.

Questions

What is the keyboard shortcut for split screen on a Mac?
On macOS 15 and later, fn control left arrow puts the front window on the left half and fn control right arrow on the right. Add shift to arrange the next window on the other half too. The same moves are in Window › Move & Resize.
What is the difference between tiling and Split View?
Tiled windows stay on your desktop with everything else. Split View puts two windows side by side in full screen, in a Space of their own. Glasspad does both: Left & Right tiles, Full Screen makes a Split View.
Does it work with two displays?
Yes. Pick the display in Arrange. If the windows are on different displays, the second one moves over first.
What if an app's window will not get narrow enough?
Some apps have a minimum width. Glasspad tells you which window will not fit instead of leaving them overlapped.
Does it leave room for Stage Manager?
Yes. Tiling keeps clear of Stage Manager's strip of apps.

Install

Glasspad is free and open source (MIT). It needs a Mac on macOS 14 or later and an iPhone on iOS 18 or later, on the same Wi-Fi. To install, you also need Xcode 26 or later (free on the App Store), a cable for the first install, and a free Apple ID. Paste this into Terminal on your Mac:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Soham109/glasspad/main/install.sh | bash

It waits for you where Apple needs a click. With a free Apple ID, apps you install yourself stop opening after 7 days; your Mac renews Glasspad over Wi-Fi before then. Every step, with pictures: Install. Help: Guide.
