Use your iPhone as a Mac trackpad

macOS has no built-in way to use an iPhone as a trackpad, so you need an app on both devices. Glasspad is a free, open source one: it turns your iPhone into a trackpad for your Mac over Wi-Fi. One finger moves the pointer, a tap clicks and you feel it, two fingers scroll and pinch, and three fingers swipe between Spaces or open Mission Control, following your Mac's own trackpad settings.

Install Glasspad Trackpad in the guide

How Glasspad does it

  • One finger moves the pointer. Tap to click, and feel a tap on every click.
  • Hold a finger still for a moment to force click. Drag, scroll with momentum, pinch, rotate and smart zoom.
  • Three fingers swipe between Spaces, or up for Mission Control.
  • Gestures and pointer speed follow your Mac's trackpad settings. Speed, scrolling and haptics can be tuned on the iPhone.
  • Touches are read at 120 Hz on ProMotion iPhones.

Want to type too? The Type layout puts the trackpad beside the keyboard on one screen.

Questions

Can I use my iPhone as a trackpad without an app?
No. Universal Control works between Macs and iPads, not iPhones, and iPhone Mirroring goes the other way: the Mac controls the iPhone. You need an app on the iPhone and a small helper on the Mac. Glasspad vs Universal Control.
Does it use Bluetooth?
No. The iPhone and the Mac talk over the same Wi-Fi network. They pair once with a QR code, and every message is encrypted.
Does it feel like a real trackpad?
It reads your Mac's trackpad settings and pointer acceleration, draws in step with the Mac's display, and keeps motion smooth over short Wi-Fi gaps. On a crowded or weak network you will notice more lag than with a Magic Trackpad.
My Mac's trackpad is broken. Can this replace it?
Once it is set up, yes. Installing it needs a few clicks on the Mac (Xcode, your Apple ID, Accessibility), so borrow a mouse for the first install.
Is it free?
Yes, all of it. There are no ads, accounts, subscriptions or in-app purchases, and the code is on GitHub under the MIT license.

Install

Glasspad is free and open source (MIT). It needs a Mac on macOS 14 or later and an iPhone on iOS 18 or later, on the same Wi-Fi. To install, you also need Xcode 26 or later (free on the App Store), a cable for the first install, and a free Apple ID. Paste this into Terminal on your Mac:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Soham109/glasspad/main/install.sh | bash

It waits for you where Apple needs a click. With a free Apple ID, apps you install yourself stop opening after 7 days; your Mac renews Glasspad over Wi-Fi before then. Every step, with pictures: Install. Help: Guide.
