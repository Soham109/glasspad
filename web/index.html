It follows your Mac's own trackpad settings.

Change a trackpad setting in System Settings and the phone follows.

Tracking speed, natural scrolling, tap to click, secondary click, three finger drag, Look Up, swipe between pages and Spaces, Mission Control, App Exposé, Launchpad, Show Desktop and click firmness all come from your Mac.

One difference: a phone screen cannot be pressed down, so a tap always clicks, even if tap to click is off on your Mac.

Prefer it different? The phone has its own speed, scrolling and haptics, and three looks: Paper, Graphite or System.

Record it once. Replay it with a tap.

In Actions, tap + and choose Record New Macro, then do the steps on your Mac. Typing becomes text, shortcuts become keystrokes and pauses become waits, in a list you can edit.

The same + adds any other app, Mac controls and your Apple Shortcuts.

It controls your Mac, so it is locked down.

Your phone and your Mac talk directly over your own Wi-Fi. There is no server in between and nothing to sign up for.

  • Pair with a QR code

    Your Mac shows a QR code holding a random secret key. The phone scans it once and keeps the key in the iOS Keychain, Apple's encrypted store for secrets. No key, no connection.

  • Encrypted, every message

    After the first hello, everything is encrypted and checked with ChaCha20-Poly1305, a widely used encryption method. Recorded traffic cannot be played back later.

  • Nothing leaves your network

    It does not work over the internet, by design. Touches go from the phone to the Mac and nowhere else. Unpaired devices on your Wi-Fi are ignored.

  • No accounts, no tracking

    No sign up, no servers, no analytics. Remove a phone from the Mac's menu and its key stops working.

Gentle on your phone.

Glasspad uses only public iOS features, the same ones any App Store app may use.

  • The screen dims after a minute without a touch.
  • It never turns your brightness up.
  • The phone only stays awake while the trackpad is on screen.
  • If the phone gets warm, it pauses haptics and background traffic and tells you calmly.

Install it with one command.

Free, but not on the App Store: real Spaces and Mission Control swipes need things Apple does not allow there. A script builds it on your Mac and puts it on your iPhone.

Terminal 
~ % curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Soham109/glasspad/main/install.sh | bash

Paste it into Terminal and press Return. It waits at each step until it sees you have done it. Safe to run again: finished steps are skipped.

You need

  • Xcode 26 or later free from the Mac App Store, a large download
  • macOS 14 or later for the Glasspad Mac app
  • iOS 18 or later on your iPhone
  • A free Apple ID
  • A USB cable for the first install
  • One Wi-Fi for the Mac and the iPhone

What happens

  1. If Xcode is missing, the App Store opens on it. Your password accepts Xcode's license and finishes its first-launch setup.

  2. Into ~/Library/Application Support/Glasspad

  3. Into ~/Projects/glasspad

  4. Sign in with your Apple ID.

    Xcode › Settings › Apple Accounts › +

    Xcode then opens the Glasspad project. Pick your name (Personal Team).

    Glasspad › Glasspad iOS › Signing & Capabilities › Team

  5. Glasspad goes into Applications and starts in the menu bar. Switch it on in the page that opens.

    System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility

  6. Plug it in, unlock it and tap Trust.

    Turn on Developer Mode. The phone restarts; unlock it, tap Turn On and enter your passcode.

    Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode

    The script builds and installs the app. Trust it once.

    Settings › General › VPN & Device Management › your Apple ID › Trust

  7. With a free Apple ID, apps you install yourself stop opening after 7 days. Your Mac checks every hour and reinstalls Glasspad over Wi-Fi once the install is 5 days old, as long as the Mac is on and the iPhone is on the same Wi-Fi at some point. It notifies you if it can't.

  8. Click the Glasspad icon in the menu bar and choose Pair Your iPhone. Scan the code with the Glasspad app or the iPhone Camera, then allow Local Network.

    The first time it shows song info, your Mac asks to let Glasspad control Spotify or Music.

Troubleshooting
The pointer does not move
Switch Glasspad off and on again in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility. Reinstalls can reset it.
The iPhone cannot find the Mac
Put both on the same Wi-Fi and allow Local Network in Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network › Glasspad.
The app will not open after a week
The Mac was off or away, so it could not renew. Run the command again.
Developer Mode is missing
Plug the iPhone in and run the command once. The setting appears after that.
Uninstall
  1. Drag Glasspad from Applications to the Trash.
  2. Delete the app on your iPhone.
  3. Turn off automatic renewal:
~/Projects/glasspad/scripts/setup-autorefresh.sh --remove
