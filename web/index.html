Glasspad

Your iPhone, as a Mac trackpad.

Wireless, with haptics, plus a dock, keyboard, media controls, macros and more. Free, private and open source.

View on GitHub Try the trackpad

Version 0.1.0, not yet released. Built from source with Xcode, for macOS 14 and iOS 17 or later.

Try it here.

Move your pointer over the phone. Click, scroll, or pinch on your trackpad. Tap a gesture below to see the rest.

It follows your Mac's own trackpad settings.

Glasspad reads your trackpad settings from macOS while it runs, so you never set anything up twice. Change one in System Settings and the phone follows.

Tracking speed, natural scrolling, tap to click, secondary click, three finger drag, Look Up, swipe between pages and Spaces, Mission Control, App Exposé, Launchpad, Show Desktop and click firmness all come from your Mac.

One difference: a phone screen cannot be pressed down, so a tap always clicks, even if tap to click is off on your Mac.

More than a trackpad.

Touch the top of the screen and a small pill appears. Pick a mode, and the phone becomes something else for a while.

Dock

  1. Dock

    Your Mac's Dock apps and running apps, as a grid. Tap one to open it or switch to it.

  2. Keys

    Type on the phone and it appears on the Mac. A row of arrow keys, Esc, Tab and Command shortcuts. Dictation works too.

  3. Media

    What is playing, with artwork. Play, pause, skip, volume and screen brightness.

  4. Deck

    A grid of your own buttons. Open an app or a link, run an Apple Shortcut, send a keystroke or run a script.

  5. Macros

    Record what you do on your Mac and replay it with one tap. Buttons change with the app in front.

  6. Clipboard

    Send what you copied on your phone to the Mac, or bring the Mac's clipboard to the phone. Text and images.

  7. Present

    Big next and previous buttons, a timer, and a laser dot you steer across the Mac's screen.

Buttons that know which app you are in.

Switch apps on your Mac and the buttons on your phone switch with it. Glasspad comes with sets for ten popular apps plus one for everywhere else, and every button is one of that app's own standard shortcuts. Edit them, or make your own.

Record it once. Replay it with a tap.

Press record and use your Mac as usual. Glasspad turns what you did into a short list of steps you can read and edit: typing becomes text, shortcuts become keystrokes, pauses become waits.

A macro can also run your Apple Shortcuts, open apps, links and files, and repeat. Start one from a button, a phone gesture or a Mac hotkey.

Scripts never run unless you turn them on for that macro. Passwords typed into secure fields are never recorded.

It controls your Mac, so it is locked down.

Your phone and your Mac talk directly over your own Wi-Fi. There is no server in between and nothing to sign up for.

  • Pair with a QR code

    Your Mac shows a QR code holding a random secret key. The phone scans it once and keeps the key in the iOS Keychain, Apple's encrypted store for secrets. No key, no connection.

  • Encrypted, every message

    After the first hello, everything is encrypted and checked with ChaCha20-Poly1305, a widely used encryption method. Recorded traffic cannot be played back later.

  • Nothing leaves your network

    It does not work over the internet, by design. Touches go from the phone to the Mac and nowhere else. Unpaired devices on your Wi-Fi are ignored.

  • No accounts, no tracking

    No sign up, no servers, no analytics. Remove a phone from the Mac's menu and its key stops working.

Gentle on your phone.

Glasspad uses only public iOS features, the same ones any App Store app may use.

  • The screen dims after a minute without a touch.
  • It never turns your brightness up.
  • The phone only stays awake while the trackpad is on screen.
  • If the phone gets warm, it pauses haptics and background traffic and tells you calmly.

Build it yourself. It is free.

Glasspad is not on the App Store. You build it with Xcode, which is what lets the Mac side do things the App Store does not allow, like real Mission Control and Spaces swipes. You need a Mac with Xcode 16 or newer, an iPhone on iOS 17 or newer, and a free Apple ID.

git clone https://github.com/Soham109/glasspad.git
cd glasspad
brew install xcodegen
xcodegen generate
open Glasspad.xcodeproj
  1. 1

    Run the Mac app

    Pick the Glasspad Mac scheme and press Run. Allow Accessibility access when asked. macOS needs it before any app can move the pointer.

  2. 2

    Put it on your iPhone

    Plug in your phone, pick the Glasspad iOS scheme and your phone, and press Run. Sign in with your Apple ID under Signing and Capabilities.

  3. 3

    Pair

    Click Glasspad in the Mac's menu bar, choose Pair, and scan the QR code with the phone.

With a free Apple ID, the iPhone app stops opening after 7 days. Run it from Xcode again to refresh it. A paid developer account lasts a year.
