Glasspad

Your iPhone, as a Mac trackpad.

Wireless, with a tap you can feel on every click. Plus a keyboard, a media remote, your Mac's apps, and buttons that follow the app in front. Free, private and open source.

View on GitHub Try the trackpad

Version 0.1.0, not yet released. Installs with one command, for macOS 14 and iOS 18 or later.

Lay it by your Mac. Touch the glass.

Your iPhone becomes a trackpad for the Mac in front of you. The pointer moves with your finger, and a small tap you can feel tells you a click landed.

It follows your Mac's own trackpad settings.

Glasspad reads your trackpad settings from macOS while it runs, so you never set anything up twice. Change one in System Settings and the phone follows.

Tracking speed, natural scrolling, tap to click, secondary click, three finger drag, Look Up, swipe between pages and Spaces, Mission Control, App Exposé, Launchpad, Show Desktop and click firmness all come from your Mac.

One difference: a phone screen cannot be pressed down, so a tap always clicks, even if tap to click is off on your Mac.

Want it to feel different from your Mac? The phone has its own pointer speed, acceleration, scroll speed and momentum, haptics you can turn on per kind, and three looks: Paper, Graphite, or System to follow your iPhone.

More than a trackpad.

Touch the pill at the top of the screen to open the mode menu. Five modes live there, plus two quick tools: Clipboard, to copy between phone and Mac, and Present, a slide remote with a timer and a laser dot. Every mode works upright or sideways.

The real thing, straight from the iPhone app: the mode menu, Apps, Keyboard, Actions, editing Pinned, the Library, the Clipboard tool and Settings.

Buttons that follow the app in front.

Switch apps on your Mac and the top of Actions switches with it. Glasspad ships sets for ten popular apps plus one for everywhere else, each built from that app's own shortcuts. Edit any of them. The things you always want sit below, in Pinned.

Record it once. Replay it with a tap.

In Actions, tap + and choose Record New Macro. Do the steps on your Mac as usual. Glasspad turns them into a short list you can read and edit, and pins the macro for you: typing becomes text, shortcuts become keystrokes, pauses become waits.

The same + Library holds Mac controls like Lock Screen, Sleep Display and Dark Mode, any app, your Apple Shortcuts, and every macro you have made.

Scripts never run unless you turn them on for that macro. Passwords typed into secure fields are never recorded.

It controls your Mac, so it is locked down.

Your phone and your Mac talk directly over your own Wi-Fi. There is no server in between and nothing to sign up for.

  • Pair with a QR code

    Your Mac shows a QR code holding a random secret key. The phone scans it once and keeps the key in the iOS Keychain, Apple's encrypted store for secrets. No key, no connection.

  • Encrypted, every message

    After the first hello, everything is encrypted and checked with ChaCha20-Poly1305, a widely used encryption method. Recorded traffic cannot be played back later.

  • Nothing leaves your network

    It does not work over the internet, by design. Touches go from the phone to the Mac and nowhere else. Unpaired devices on your Wi-Fi are ignored.

  • No accounts, no tracking

    No sign up, no servers, no analytics. Remove a phone from the Mac's menu and its key stops working.

Gentle on your phone.

Glasspad uses only public iOS features, the same ones any App Store app may use.

  • The screen dims after a minute without a touch.
  • It never turns your brightness up.
  • The phone only stays awake while the trackpad is on screen.
  • If the phone gets warm, it pauses haptics and background traffic and tells you calmly.

One command. Free.

Glasspad is not on the App Store, because real Mission Control and Spaces swipes need things Apple does not allow there. Instead, one command builds it on your Mac and puts it on your iPhone. You need a Mac with Xcode (free from the App Store), an iPhone on iOS 18 or newer, a cable for the first install, and a free Apple ID.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Soham109/glasspad/main/install.sh | bash

Paste it into Terminal. It walks you through each step, does everything it can by itself, and waits whenever Apple needs you to click or tap. Safe to run again: finished steps are skipped.

  1. 1

    Sets up the Mac

    Checks Xcode, fetches the build tools and the source, then installs the menu bar app. You allow Accessibility once, so it can move the pointer.

  2. 2

    Installs on your iPhone

    Sign in with your Apple ID in Xcode, plug in your phone, and turn on Developer Mode when asked. The script builds and installs the app.

  3. 3

    Keeps it installed

    A free Apple ID makes iPhone apps stop opening after 7 days. Your Mac renews Glasspad over Wi-Fi before that, by itself.

Then pair: click Glasspad in the Mac's menu bar, choose Pair new iPhone, and scan the code with the phone.
