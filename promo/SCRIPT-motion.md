# Glasspad motion film: script

A 2D motion film, 1920 x 1080 draft and 3840 x 2160 master, 60 fps, about 87 seconds. The
picture is drawn in the website's visual language (web/src): flat devices, real menu bars, the
app's own screens. Every scene shows one feature as one action on the iPhone and its result on
the Mac. The voice says what the picture cannot: one idea a scene, never a caption read aloud.

Voice: ElevenLabs, eleven_v4, Sarah. Pace 140 to 155 words a minute, measured per line in
`src/motion/vo-measured.json`. Music: "Uplifting Electronic Indie" by WinnieTheMoog, CC BY 4.0
(MUSIC.md), cut on its bar grid (145 BPM, a bar is 1.655 s). Scenes start on downbeats.

| 1 | 0 to 4 | Open | A dot; it moves and the Mac's pointer moves with it. Pull back: a MacBook, an iPhone on a stand. A tap opens Safari. "A control surface for your Mac." | The iPhone in your pocket, made part of your Mac. |

| 6 | 22 to 25 | Layouts | The phone turns back; the screen splits into Trackpad and Actions; the divider (the dot) snaps to a third; Media folds in as a third pane. | Or put two or three side by side. |
| 7 | 25 to 30 | Remote | Dark room, a TV. A swipe skips 10 seconds, a swipe up raises the volume, Search types into the TV's search box. | From the couch, it's a remote. |
| 8 | 30 to 33 | Present | Slides on a screen. The timer runs; the dot turns red and becomes the laser; Next turns the slide. | On stage, it keeps time and points for you. |

| 10 | 37 to 40 | StandBy | Night. Clock beside Now Playing; the lamp goes out and the screen turns dim red. | At night, it's a clock on your nightstand. |
| 11 | 40 to 43 | Themes | One screen, six themes on the beat: Paper, Mono, Glass, Aurora, Midnight, Photo. | Twenty-eight themes, to make it yours. |

| 13 | 47 to 51 | End | Everything folds back into the dot; the dot becomes the Glasspad mark. "A control surface for your Mac, on your iPhone." "Free and open source." The address. The music credit. | Glasspad. Free and open source. |

Feature names appear as small captions (a dot in the scene's accent, then the name). The only
other words on screen are the opening line, the privacy line and the end card.
