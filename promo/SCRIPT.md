# Glasspad film: script

One idea carries the film: the dot under your finger. It is the app icon, the thing you see on
the phone, and on the Mac it becomes the pointer. Every big transition is that dot moving,
growing or splitting.

- Length: 77.5 s (film), 28 s (vertical teaser).
- Music: "Brand New World" by Kai Engel (CC BY 4.0), 120 BPM. One bar = 2 s = 120 frames.
  Each 8 s phrase is four bars; the fourth bar is quieter, which is where transitions breathe.
  See MUSIC.md for the edit.
- Voice: one narrator, warm and unhurried. Short sentences. Nothing the picture already says.
- Never on screen or in the voice: latency figures, invented numbers, dashes, emoji.

## Arc

| # | Time | Phrase | What we feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 to 8 | Hook | A small, familiar annoyance |
| 1 | 8 to 16 | Reveal | Oh, it's the phone |
| 2 | 16 to 24 | Touch | It actually feels like a trackpad |
| 3 | 24 to 32 | Spaces | Wow, it follows my fingers |
| 4 | 32 to 40 | Settings | It already knows me |
| 5 | 40 to 48 | Modes, Apps | It is more than a trackpad |
| 6 | 48 to 56 | Actions | It is paying attention to what I do |
| 7 | 56 to 64 | Media | It is fun |
| 8 | 64 to 72 | Privacy, CTA | I can trust it, and it is free |
| | 72 to 77.5 | End card | Where to get it |

## Voice

```
HOOK        Your Mac is over there.
            Your phone is right here.
REVEAL      Glasspad turns it into a trackpad.
TOUCH       Slide a finger, and the pointer glides, in step with your display.
            Tap, and you feel the click.
SPACES      Swipe three fingers, and your Spaces move with your hand.
SETTINGS    It follows your Mac's own trackpad settings, so it already feels like yours.
MODES       Tap the top for everything else.
APPS        Open Word without getting up.
ACTIONS     Actions follow the app in front.
            Switch to Safari, and the buttons switch with you.
MEDIA       Play Spotify on your Mac, and scrub it from your phone.
PRIVACY     No account. No cloud.
            Every touch is encrypted, and stays on your Wi-Fi.
END         Glasspad is free and open source.
```

## Picture

### 0. Hook (0 to 8). Music: sparse piano.
A wide, quiet frame. Far away in the upper right, a Mac on a desk, small. Words build in
per word with a soft upward mask: "Your Mac is over there." The camera does not move to it.
Second line: a phone slides up into the foreground, huge, close to the lens, slightly out of
the Mac's plane (parallax: the Mac drifts left as the phone rises). "Your phone is right here."
On "here", a single dot lands on the phone's glass.

### 1. Reveal (8 to 16). Music: the first full bar lands at 8.0.
Hit on 8.0: the dot expands into a circular mask that wipes the phone's screen to the
trackpad surface (real recording). The phone tips back 70 degrees and lies down in 3D, turning
sideways, next to a full-size Mac that slides in from the right. "Glasspad" sets letter by
letter above, then shrinks into a lockup and leaves on bar 4.

### 2. Touch (16 to 24).
Close on the glass (real recording, one finger gliding). Match cut: the finger dot slides off
the phone's edge and becomes the macOS pointer on the Mac. The pointer glides in a smooth arc;
a thin ruler of display frames ticks under it, one tick per frame, labelled "120 Hz" (the only
number, and it is the display's). Tap: the dot squashes, a ring pulses out on the phone, and
the phone body gives a 2 px shake (the haptic, made visible). A Finder icon on the Mac opens.

### 3. Spaces (24 to 32). The first wow.
Three dots on the phone. As they move left, the whole Mac desktop moves with them, one to one,
not a cut: the next Space slides in following the fingers, overshoots slightly, settles.
Then back, halfway, and released. Kinetic word "with" under the line, tracking the fingers.

### 4. Settings (32 to 40).
The Mac's System Settings Trackpad pane flies in as a card in 3D. Three toggles
(Tap to click, Natural scrolling, Three finger drag) flip one by one; each flip sends a small
dot along a curve to the phone, which shows the same value. The phone and Mac pane converge
and stack: same settings, one source.

### 5. Modes and Apps (40 to 48). Music lifts.
Tap the pill at the top of the phone (real recording): the mode menu opens. Camera pushes into
the phone. "Apps" (real recording): the Mac's apps at full resolution. A tap on Word; the dot
leaves the phone and becomes the Word icon bouncing in the Mac's Dock; a Word window scales
up from the Dock.

### 6. Actions (48 to 56). The second wow.
Split frame: Mac left, phone right, both large. The Mac's front app is Word, the phone's
Actions screen shows Word buttons. Click Safari on the Mac: the phone's top section re-lays
itself into Safari buttons on its own (real recording, driven by a scripted front app switch).
Below it, pinned actions; their labels flick in as kinetic type: Screenshot. Lock. Dark mode.
Dark mode is pressed: the Mac's whole UI inverts to dark in a circular wipe from the button.

### 7. Media (56 to 64). Music: full return.
The Mac is playing music (Spotify window, flat). The phone shows Media (real recording):
artwork, title, controls. A finger scrubs; the Mac's progress bar follows. The artwork
blooms into a full-frame colour field (flat), the beat lands, cut.

### 8. Privacy and end (64 to 77.5).
Pull out wide: phone and Mac inside a thin rounded rectangle labelled "Your Wi-Fi". A single
line between them, with a lock that closes. Words "No account." "No cloud." strike in and out
on the beat. The rectangle shrinks, the devices fade, the line collapses into the dot. The dot
glides once, the way it does on the icon, and the end card sets:

```
Glasspad
Your iPhone, as a Mac trackpad.
Free and open source. MIT.
github.com/Soham109/glasspad
glasspad.sohamaggarwal.com
```

## Vertical teaser (28 s, its own edit)

Phone-first and vertical: the phone fills the frame, the Mac is a window above it.

```
0 to 4     "Your Mac is over there. Your phone is right here."  (type stacked, large)
4 to 12    Reveal hit. "Glasspad turns it into a trackpad."  Spaces swipe follows the fingers.
12 to 20   Actions switch on their own; Open Word.
20 to 28   "Glasspad is free and open source."  End card.
```

## Rules this script keeps

- Each line shows something new. No line restates the picture or another line.
- The product name is spoken twice: the reveal and the end.
- "Your" is the only word that repeats on purpose.
